| 2026-08-22 18:52:00 CST | Completed the final title, relationship, literary-source, ecology, medievalism, and worldbuilding audit. Retitled the artifact **The World Beneath the War: Material Reality in Westeros and Middle-earth**, replacing the generic AI-analysis label **George R.R. Martin Deconstructed** while retaining the stable canonical slug. Rejected the inherited checklist standard that treated missing soil pH, exact crop yields, irrigation specifications, firewood tonnage, or similar technical inventory as evidence of shallow worldbuilding. Rebuilt the comparison around **causal integration**: whether material conditions change the feasible actions of people and institutions, whether societies develop responses to recurring constraints, whether consequences propagate beyond a scene, and what symbolic or historical load the material world carries. Corrected the inherited claim that Martin leaves winter and food largely static: Martin's archived correspondence explicitly describes preserved food, granaries, fishing, Winterfell's glass gardens, false springs, maester planting advice, migration, famine, and starvation under long winters, while the novels repeatedly make food, war damage, geography, travel, scarcity, and provisioning politically consequential. Narrowed the surviving criticism to incomplete system closure: societies exposed for centuries to stochastic multi-year seasons would plausibly display more visible demographic, fuel, livestock, seed, trade, urban, and resilience institutions than the published narrative consistently models. Reframed Martin's famous Aragorn tax-policy remark as an argument that moral legitimacy does not remove hard administrative choices, scarcity, and unintended consequences, not as a promise to simulate modern tax bureaucracy. Added medievalism scholarship to distinguish Westeros's powerful historical texture from literal medieval accuracy. Recast Tolkien as a different kind of material worldbuilder rather than a superior agronomic simulator: Middle-earth does not quantify agricultural systems, but landscape repeatedly carries historical, moral, and narrative agency through Fangorn, Isengard, Mordor, the Shire, destruction, stewardship, and restoration. Added Tolkien ecocritical scholarship and bounded winter-ecology/logistics research as stress tests rather than as attempts to scientifically debunk fantasy. Removed escaped conversion citations, raw comparison/flowchart residue, weak fan/wiki source chains, unsupported technical completeness requirements, and the inherited one-dimensional realism ranking.
